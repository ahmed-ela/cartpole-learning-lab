# Frozen-checkpoint diagnosis for seed 162066966

Run `node experiments/curve-diagnosis/evaluation/diagnose.mjs`, then `confirm.mjs`, then `center-holdout.mjs`, from the repository root. All use the byte-for-byte PPO snapshot from `experiments/curve-analysis/rl.snapshot.mjs`; the original application source is read-only.

Training replay was verified against the original ensemble: all 80 recent20 training scores and all completed episode scores match exactly. `replay-verification.json` records the original training history. `checkpoints.json` preserves actor and critic weights plus Adam m/v/t state at checkpoints 0, 8 through 30, 40, and 80. Network architecture and all training defaults remain unchanged.

`eval.mjs` is a reusable evaluation library. `createDataset({count,seed,maxSteps})` gives each episode a SHA-256-derived seed, draws four reset-state coordinates uniformly in [-0.05, 0.05], then precomputes 500 action uniforms. Every checkpoint uses exactly the same reset state and the same uniform at each timestep. Episode duration therefore cannot shift another episode's random state. `evaluatePolicy(s => agent.probability(s),dataset)` preserves the app's sampled binary action distribution. Deterministic argmax evaluations are recorded separately. `pairedDifference(before.episodes,after.episodes)` estimates after-minus-before return with paired standard error.

The main cohort has 1,500 fresh episodes per checkpoint, using dataset seed `cartpole-diagnosis-independent-v1`. Evaluation took 9.592 seconds. Running the script generates each `checkpoint-{update}-episodes.json` with full episode outcome arrays for sampled and deterministic policies. Fields include initial and final state, duration/return, cart/pole/both/cap cause, failure side, angle and position summaries, velocity summaries, and near-boundary activity. It also generates `trajectory-examples.json` with full trajectories for representative median-duration episodes by cause and `dataset.json` with per-episode seeds, initial states, and hashes of the precomputed action uniforms. These bulk files are ignored by Git; the compact summaries and checkpoint weights are committed.

`summary.json` contains checkpoint means, standard errors, approximate 95% confidence intervals, capped500 fractions, physical failure counts/durations, and paired adjacent-checkpoint differences. Mean intervals use the sample standard error and normal approximation, justified here as an approximation to a mean over many independent episodes; this does not assume individual returns or learning curves are Gaussian. Cause/cap intervals use Wilson intervals. An empirical mean interval of [500,500] at a checkpoint where every tested episode reaches the cap does not prove there are no unobserved failures; use the cap-rate Wilson interval for that uncertainty. These evaluation intervals describe reset/action randomness for one frozen policy, not training-seed uncertainty.

The large physical performance loss occurs earlier than the displayed rolling-mean dip. Frozen sampled-policy means at updates 14, 15, and 16 were 329.804, 181.215, and 165.369. At update 15, 1,422/1,500 episodes failed only at the cart boundary, all to the left; their mean pole angle at failure was 4.18 degrees, well below the 12-degree limit. By update 18, the mean recovered to 316.611 and cart failures were more balanced left/right. At update 20 the mean fell again to 197.515, now with 1,419/1,420 cart-only failures to the right. Deterministic-policy means also declined from 487.188 at update 14 to 200.807 and 184.188 at updates 15 and 16. Action sampling noise alone cannot account for that collapse.

The displayed recent20 training mean peaked at update 16 and reached its local trough at update 19, while frozen-policy return was already recovering. The measured fixed16-to19 frozen-policy difference was +118.745 (paired95% CI +112.967 to +124.523). The training window mixes episodes collected under earlier policies; it is lagged evidence of those policies and their episode draws, not an immediate score of the policy just updated.

After examining the main cohort, checkpoint comparisons were fixed before collecting a separate 2,000-episode holdout cohort (`cartpole-diagnosis-holdout-v1`). Running `confirm.mjs` generates `holdout-raw.json` with all outcomes; the committed `holdout-summary.json` records comparisons. The holdout confirms update14-to15 loss of 146.8025 points (paired95% CI 142.3396 to 151.2654), update14-to16 loss of 163.326 points (158.8709 to 167.7811), and update16-to18 recovery of 153.272 points (148.5887 to 157.9553).

A predeclared holdout test changes only the constant zero-state logit: use `sigmoid(z(s)-z([0,0,0,0]))` for both checkpoint14 and15. This is an inference-time controller intervention, not further training. Original holdout means were 328.5165 and 181.714; centered means were 350.8995 and 347.425. The centered checkpoint change was -3.4745 (95% CI -7.1623 to +0.2133), versus original -146.8025. The paired interaction `(centered15-centered14)-(original15-original14)` was +143.328 (137.7956 to 148.8604), so about 97.6% of that original mean drop disappeared. At centered15, cart-only failures split 682 left / 862 right and 418/2,000 episodes reached the cap. The committed `holdout-validation.json` records the complete contrast and limitations; running `center-holdout.mjs` generates `centered-holdout-raw.json` with raw centered outcomes and difference arrays.

This intervention identifies the changed constant logit as a major causal component of this particular drop on these reset/action distributions. It does not by itself establish every optimizer-level cause or make logit centering a universally optimal fix; the centered controllers remain imperfect. The later reversal of cart drift also shows why zero-state right-action probability must not be equated directly with the eventual direction of cart drift in the coupled cart/pole dynamics.
